5.2     Flow Matching Framework

Both Score SDEs (Chapter 4) and NODEs (Section 5.1) describe generative
modeling as a continuous transport process. Score SDEs formulate this transport
through stochastic dynamics together with an associated PF-ODE, whereas NODEs
directly parameterize a deterministic ODE. In the standard generative setting,
both aim to transport a simple Gaussian prior pprior into the data distribution
pdata .
    A practical limitation of NODEs is that likelihood-based training requires
repeatedly solving an ODE. The Flow Matching (FM) framework (Lipman et al.,
2022; Lipman et al., 2024; Tong et al., 2024) avoids this cost by training a neural
network to predict the velocity field directly through a regression objective. ODE
integration is therefore needed for generation, but not inside the training loop,
making FM training simulation-free.
    More generally, FM is not restricted to Gaussian-to-data transport. It considers
a source distribution psrc and a target distribution ptgt , both assumed to be easy
to sample from. Standard generative modeling is recovered as the special case

                               psrc = pprior ,      ptgt = pdata ,

where pprior is typically Gaussian.
    In this section, we adopt the FM viewpoint3 Its goal is to learn a time-dependent
velocity field vt (xt ) whose associated ODE transports samples along a prescribed
probability path {pt }t∈[0,1] satisfying

                                  p0 = psrc ,       p1 = ptgt .

When psrc is Gaussian, we refer to this setting as Gaussian Flow Matching. Thus,
FM provides both a simulation-free training method for generative modeling and
a general framework for learning continuous transport between arbitrary endpoint
distributions.

                            vt (xt ) := Ez∼p(·|xt ) [vt (xt |z)] .               (5.2.9)

identity in Equation (5.2.3).

                                                h                        i

                                   v∗ (xt , t) = vt (xt ).                      (5.2.11)
In other words, although each training example uses a conditional velocity vt (xt |z)
as its target, averaging over these examples recovers the desired marginal velocity
